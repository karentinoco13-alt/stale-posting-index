Know the catch before you rely on this
  • Not live yet. The app doesn't pull jobs from the web on its own yet. Rankings use only the postings you add, files you upload and community reports, so a company missing from this list isn't proof it hires fairly.
  • Coverage is partial. Many large companies and most small and local employers aren't tracked yet, and job sites like Indeed and LinkedIn don't share their data.
  • Open a long time isn't always bad. Some roles are evergreen, seasonal, or have many openings under one title.
  • Reports aren't verified. Community reports, flags and comments are people's own experiences.
  • Checks can be wrong. The fraud and red-flag checks look for common patterns. They can miss a scam or flag a real job.

    How it scores

    1. Same job. Postings are grouped into one role by company and a cleaned-up job title. Case, punctuation, requisition numbers and tags like "(Remote)" are ignored, and "Sr." counts as "Senior".
    2. Days open. From the earliest posting of that role to its last-seen date, or to the as-of date if it's still posted. Reposting doesn't reset the clock.
    3. Rank. By default, companies are ranked by the total days their roles have stayed open beyond 3 months.
    4. Serial reposter. Flagged when any role has been posted 3 or more times, for example taken down and put back up under a new ID or with a refreshed date.
    5. A signal, not proof. A long-open posting can also be an evergreen or high-volume role. Use the ranking as a reason to ask questions.

    Add postings

    Saw the same job reposted with a new date? Add it again with the new date. The app counts each one as a repost and flags companies that do it repeatedly.

    Copy rows from a spreadsheet or job search and paste them here. The first line must be column names: company, title, posted_date, and optionally industry, location, last_seen, url. Commas or tabs both work.

    Most likely to be a fraud

    Got a recruiter email or job message that feels off? Paste it here with its From line. The checker looks for common job-scam signs, such as a free email address for a big company, requests for money or bank details, or interviews over chat apps. Then it ranks every email you've checked, most likely fraud first. Emails stay in this browser.

    Or check a job posting for signs an employer may take advantage of workers, which is common at some small clinics, home-health agencies, contractors and staffing firms: fees to start, commission-only pay, unpaid "working interviews", contractor (1099) status for a full-time schedule, no pay listed, mandatory overtime, or "always hiring".

    Heads up: these checks look for common warning patterns. They can miss a scam or flag a real job. Never send money or personal details until you've confirmed the job on the employer's official site.

    Community reports

    Seen a company keep the same job posted for more than 3 months? Report it so others know. If you've seen the same thing, flag the report, and add a comment about your experience. Everyone who can open this app sees reports, flags and comments, without your name.

    Heads up: reports aren't verified. Treat them as leads to check, not as facts about a company.

    Keep it factual. Say what was posted, when you first saw it, and what happened when you applied. Don't name individual people, share personal information, or insult anyone. Reports are people's experiences, not verified facts. Anything that breaks these rules is removed.

    About this project

    Built so job seekers can tell a real opening from a posting that never closes, and so hiring teams can see where the tech talent market is stuck. Postings come from what you add or from the optional collector, which checks tech career sites such as Apple, Microsoft, NVIDIA, Amazon, Blue Origin and Tesla once a day and exports a CSV for the Add postings tab.
